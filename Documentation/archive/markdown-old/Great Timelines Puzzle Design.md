__Great Puzzle Design__

__What Makes a Great Timelines Puzzle__

__This document defines the qualities, structure, tone, and psychological design behind what makes a *great* Timelines puzzle\. It is intended to guide puzzle generation \(via GPT or manually\), help with quality control, and inform future features like editing, rating, and surfacing user\-generated puzzles\.__

__🔹 Core Design Goals__

__A great Timelines puzzle should:__

- __Make players feel smart and surprised  
  
__
- __Evoke nostalgia, emotional memory, or cultural recognition  
  
__
- __Spark delight, curiosity, or learning \(even in failure\)  
  
__
- __Feel cohesive as a set, not just a random assortment  
  
__
- __Reward both casual players and subject\-matter enthusiasts  
  
__

__✏️ Puzzle Format__

__Each puzzle consists of 10 chronological cards\.  
 Each card includes:__

- __label: short, compelling headline \(required\)  
  
__
- __date: year \(required, integer\)  
  
__
- __tooltip\.description: short context paragraph \(optional\)  
  
__
- __tooltip\.quote: direct quote \(optional\)  
  
__
- __media: Deezer trackId, image, or video embed \(optional\)  
  
__

__✨ What Makes a Card Great__

__A great card is one or more of the following:__

- __Recognizable: "The iPhone is unveiled"  
  
__
- __Emotionally loaded: "Googoosh performs after decades in exile"  
  
__
- __Culturally sticky: "The Macarena takes over the world"  
  
__
- __Unexpected but true: "The fork sparks controversy in France"  
  
__
- __Useful as a clue: lets the player *reason* about when it happened  
  
__

__Label Style:__

- __Use headline\-style language, not academic phrasing  
  
__
- __Embrace pop tone where appropriate \("Barbie debuts," not "Mattel releases the Barbie doll"\)  
  
__
- __Use real\-world terms \("The Beatles go full psychedelic"\)  
  
__
- __Avoid passive voice, dry syntax, or encyclopedia tone  
  
__

__🧐 Difficulty Balance__

__A great puzzle should:__

- __Contain 10 cards, always  
  
__
- __The date needs to be precisely defined\. For example we should never say something like “the golden age of cinema in America” because that spans many years\. An acceptable card would be “\[Movie Name\] helps usher in golden age of Cinema in US” because it gets the same point across but ties the event to a particular date\. We may want to add date ranges at some point in the future \(but would need to be careful not to create too much overlap in a given puzzle\)\.__

  


- __Have a balance of difficulty:  
  
__
	- __Easy: 4–5 cards the average player can confidently place  
  
__
	- __Medium: 3–4 cards that require reasoning  
  
__
	- __Hard: 1–2 cards that surprise or challenge the player  
  
__
- __Avoid clustering all dates within the same decade \(unless thematically appropriate\)  
  
__

__Easy Cards:__

- __Highly recognizable cultural touchstones  
  
__
- __Often associated with major milestones, e\.g\. moon landing, iPhone, Titanic  
  
__

__Medium Cards:__

- __Familiar but more specific, e\.g\. "Shrek wins first Best Animated Feature Oscar"  
  
__
- __Things that require triangulation \(e\.g\. did it come before or after X?\)  
  
__

__Hard Cards:__

- __Less\-known but delightful facts, e\.g\. "The printing press is banned by the Pope"  
  
__
- __Useful to teach players something unexpected  
  
__
