Great Puzzle Design

What Makes a Great Timelines Puzzle

This document defines the qualities, structure, tone, and psychological design behind what makes a great Timelines puzzle. It is intended to guide puzzle generation (via GPT or manually), help with quality control, and inform future features like editing, rating, and surfacing user-generated puzzles.



🔹 Core Design Goals

A great Timelines puzzle should:

Make players feel smart and surprised

Evoke nostalgia, emotional memory, or cultural recognition

Spark delight, curiosity, or learning (even in failure)

Feel cohesive as a set, not just a random assortment

Reward both casual players and subject-matter enthusiasts



✏️ Puzzle Format

Each puzzle consists of 10 chronological cards. Each card includes:

label: short, compelling headline (required)

date: year (required, integer)

tooltip.description: short context paragraph (optional)

tooltip.quote: direct quote (optional)

media: Deezer trackId, image, or video embed (optional)



✨ What Makes a Card Great

A great card is one or more of the following:

Recognizable: "The iPhone is unveiled"

Emotionally loaded: "Googoosh performs after decades in exile"

Culturally sticky: "The Macarena takes over the world"

Unexpected but true: "The fork sparks controversy in France"

Useful as a clue: lets the player reason about when it happened

Label Style:

Use headline-style language, not academic phrasing

Embrace pop tone where appropriate ("Barbie debuts," not "Mattel releases the Barbie doll")

Use real-world terms ("The Beatles go full psychedelic")

Avoid passive voice, dry syntax, or encyclopedia tone



🧐 Difficulty Balance

A great puzzle should:

Contain 10 cards, always

The date needs to be precisely defined. For example we should never say something like “the golden age of cinema in America” because that spans many years. An acceptable card would be “[Movie Name] helps usher in golden age of Cinema in US” because it gets the same point across but ties the event to a particular date. We may want to add date ranges at some point in the future (but would need to be careful not to create too much overlap in a given puzzle).



Have a balance of difficulty:

Easy: 4–5 cards the average player can confidently place

Medium: 3–4 cards that require reasoning

Hard: 1–2 cards that surprise or challenge the player

Avoid clustering all dates within the same decade (unless thematically appropriate)

Easy Cards:

Highly recognizable cultural touchstones

Often associated with major milestones, e.g. moon landing, iPhone, Titanic

Medium Cards:

Familiar but more specific, e.g. "Shrek wins first Best Animated Feature Oscar"

Things that require triangulation (e.g. did it come before or after X?)

Hard Cards:

Less-known but delightful facts, e.g. "The printing press is banned by the Pope"

Useful to teach players something unexpected
