# Timelines Party Mode: Complete Guide

## Overview

Party Mode transforms Timelines from a personal puzzle-solving experience into a **collaborative social game** designed for groups of 2-8 players. While maintaining the core timeline mechanics, Party Mode emphasizes **group discussion, debate, and collective decision-making** to create memorable social experiences.

## Core Party Mode Concept

### Group Timeline Building
Instead of individual players building their own timelines, **the entire group works together** to build a single shared timeline. Every decision becomes a group discussion, every placement sparks debate, and every reveal creates shared moments of triumph or learning.

### Conversation-Driven Gameplay
Party Mode is designed to **maximize social interaction**:
- **Group deliberation** before each card placement
- **Friendly debate** about timing and cultural context  
- **Collaborative reasoning** using everyone's knowledge
- **Shared celebration** of correct guesses and learning from mistakes

## How Party Mode Works

### Setup & Group Formation
- **2-8 players** gather around a single screen/device
- Players can form **teams** or play as one large group
- Each round uses a **curated timeline** designed for group discussion
- **Moderator role** can rotate or be assigned to facilitate discussion

### Core Mechanics (Same as Single Player)
- Start with one **grey anchor card** pre-placed on the timeline
- Receive one **draggable card** to place relative to existing cards
- **Drag and drop** the card before or after other cards on the timeline
- Cards turn **green (correct)** or **red (incorrect)** after placement
- **Correct dates** are always displayed after placement
- Timeline grows more challenging with each additional card

### Party Mode Differences

#### 1. **Group Decision Making**
- **Discussion time** before each placement (30-60 seconds recommended)
- **Consensus building** - group must agree on placement
- **Voting system** option when group can't reach consensus
- **Time limits** to keep energy high and prevent over-analysis

#### 2. **Social Scoring System**
- **Team celebration** for correct placements
- **Group learning** from incorrect placements with detailed explanations
- **Collective progress** tracking rather than individual scores
- **Bonus points** for interesting facts shared during discussion

#### 3. **Enhanced Explanations**
- **Detailed context** provided after each card placement
- **Discussion prompts** to encourage conversation about cultural impact
- **"Did you know?" facts** that spark additional conversation
- **Historical connections** between cards already placed

#### 4. **Flexible Difficulty**
- **Adaptive challenge** based on group performance and engagement
- **Knowledge pooling** means groups can tackle harder timelines
- **Mixed expertise** creates natural teaching moments
- **Scalable complexity** for different group dynamics

## Gameplay Flow

### Round Structure

#### **Phase 1: Card Reveal (15 seconds)**
- New draggable card is revealed to the group
- Initial reactions and recognition
- Quick knowledge sharing about the item

#### **Phase 2: Group Discussion (30-60 seconds)**
- **Open debate** about when the item belongs
- **Knowledge sharing** from different group members
- **Reasoning together** using visual clues and cultural context
- **Building consensus** on placement strategy

#### **Phase 3: Placement Decision (15 seconds)**
- **Final decision** on where to place the card
- **Designated placer** or rotating responsibility
- **Group confirmation** before final drop

#### **Phase 4: Reveal & Learning (30 seconds)**
- **Scoring reveal** - green for correct, red for incorrect
- **Actual date display** and additional context
- **Group reaction** - celebration or learning moment
- **Interesting facts** and cultural context sharing

### Example Round Walkthrough

**Timeline so far**: "Moon Landing" (1969, anchor card)

**New Card**: Screenshot from "Jaws" movie

**Discussion Phase**:
- *Player 1*: "That's definitely Jaws, I think that was early 70s?"
- *Player 2*: "My dad always talks about seeing it in theaters, he graduated in '75..."
- *Player 3*: "The special effects look pretty advanced for the early 70s"
- *Player 4*: "I remember it being the first summer blockbuster"

**Consensus**: Place "Jaws" after "Moon Landing" 

**Reveal**: ✅ Correct! "Jaws" (1975)

**Learning Moment**: "Jaws was released in 1975 and is credited with creating the summer blockbuster phenomenon. It was the highest-grossing film of all time until Star Wars in 1977."
